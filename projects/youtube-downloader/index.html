Project Story

YTGrab evolved from a simple download script into a deliberate desktop workflow moving from "quick paste-and-save" to "preview, configure, queue, and batch process" with full visibility.

The Challenge

Typical Downloaders Leave Users Guessing

Most YouTube downloaders hide format options, treat playlists as all-or-nothing, and provide minimal feedback when downloads fail. Users paste a link, click download, and hope for the best with no preview, no batch control, and no recovery context.

  • No preview or review step before starting
  • Limited playlist control and weak batch support
  • Poor error context and no download history
The Vision

A Workflow That Feels Predictable

Build a desktop app that feels reliable and controlled load media metadata first, let users choose formats and quality levels, allow queue-based batching, and keep both progress and history visible throughout the entire workflow.

  • Preview media details before committing
  • Make queuing the standard workflow
  • Surface progress, history, and theme controls
The Result

Queue-First Downloads With Full Visibility

YTGrab reads real yt-dlp metadata, exposes video and audio format options, lets users add multiple items to a queue with full context, then processes everything together with live progress tracking and persistent history for easy recovery.

  • Fetch all available formats and resolutions
  • Queue items store title, thumbnail, mode, quality
  • Live progress shows speed, size, and batch status

Application Limitations

YTGrab's core functionality depends entirely on a valid cookies.txt file exported from your browser. This creates significant friction because YouTube frequently invalidates these cookies through various mechanisms the app cannot control.

🍪 Cookie Dependency

Every download requires fresh browser cookies. When YouTube detects account re-authentication, security checks, region changes, or anti-bot signals, your cookies expire and downloads fail immediately even though the app code is working correctly.

🔧 Constant Maintenance

Users must repeatedly export cookies from their browser, manually replace the file, and restart the app whenever YouTube invalidates credentials. This makes the tool high-maintenance rather than "set and forget," especially burdensome for non-technical users.

⚠ Unpredictable Failures

Startup checks can fail without warning, queued downloads get interrupted mid-batch, and the same workflow that succeeded yesterday may suddenly break today. Troubleshooting becomes harder because failures often stem from external platform changes rather than actual bugs.

🔒 Security and Trust Concerns

Handling sensitive authentication data manually increases both complexity and risk. Users must store browser session cookies locally, trust the app with account access, and accept that mistakes in cookie management can expose credentials or lock them out of downloads entirely.

Even with careful UI design and clear error messaging, the app's reliability is fundamentally limited by this external dependency. Long-term stability remains weaker than solutions that avoid requiring continuous cookie renewal.
