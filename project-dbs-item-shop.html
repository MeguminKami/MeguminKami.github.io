DBS Item Shop

DBS Item Shop was developed for the SIE UC course as a PHP web application for the Djabusabi group: an online store with authentication, searchable catalog pages, product detail screens, cart and checkout flows, order history, and administrator tools for products and users.

Overview

DBS Item Shop started from the idea of creating a website for the Djabusabi group that could represent its identity and activities. In the delivered course version, that idea takes the form of an online store where users can browse products, authenticate, manage personal data, place orders, and move through a structured shopping flow.

The store is the central delivered implementation for SIE, but the website was designed as a base that could grow with new Djabusabi-related features later on. That broader direction is what gives the project its mix of customer journeys, administration, reusable templates, and a structured database model.

Project Breakdown

DBS Item Shop goes beyond a simple product grid by combining group identity, customer shopping flows, and administrator responsibility inside one web application.

⚠ Theme

The project theme was to build a website for the Djabusabi group. In the SIE coursework version, that identity is expressed through an online store that could later grow into a broader community website.

  • The store is the present implementation, not the limit of the concept.
  • The website is meant to represent both identity and activity.
  • Reusable templates and shared structure support that broader direction.
💡 Customer

On the customer side, the application includes login and signup, product search, product detail pages, cart management, checkout, and order history as the core shopping journey.

  • Search includes price, alphabetical ordering, and product-type filters.
  • Product pages expose image, description, price, stock, and quantity selection.
  • Checkout progresses through delivery, payment, and confirmation.
✅ Admin

The administrator scope is equally important: product maintenance, user maintenance, and access to dedicated management pages are all part of the original application.

  • Administrators can add, edit, and remove products from the catalog.
  • They can also list, add, edit, and remove users.
  • The project scope also leaves room for ticket-based support interactions.

Development Plan

The implementation was divided into four phases, with responsibilities shared between the two authors across data, authentication, administration, catalog, and cart/order features.

Phase 01 - 16/12-22/12

Base structure, navigation, login, and signup

Joao Ferreira handled the base database structure and initial navigation, while Joao Pessoa worked on authentication and the signup flow with validation.

Phase 02 - 23/12-30/12

User management and administration

The second phase focused on adding users through the database, listing users, removing users, and editing usernames, emails, and permissions.

Phase 03 - 31/12-09/01

Catalog search, product detail, and product CRUD

This phase delivered product search with filters and ordering, product detail pages, catalog insertion, and administrator editing/removal of store items.

Phase 04 - 10/01-24/01

Cart, orders, and ticket interface

The final phase covered adding products to the cart, cart management, the order-history page, and a ticket interface for additional user support.
